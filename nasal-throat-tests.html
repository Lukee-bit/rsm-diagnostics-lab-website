What the Upper Respiratory Micro Screen (Nasal & Throat) includes

Culture recovers and identifies the bacterial and fungal organisms colonizing the nasal passages and throat, with MARCoNS evaluation and isolate identification to inform clinical decisions.

Bacterial culture

Culture of nasal and throat specimens to recover and identify clinically significant bacteria and characterize what is present in the sample.

Fungal culture

Culture for yeast and mold to detect fungal organisms that may contribute to persistent or recurrent upper respiratory symptoms.

MARCoNS evaluation

MARCoNS testing is performed by culture. Recovered isolates are evaluated for MARCoNS (Multiple Antibiotic-Resistant Coagulase-Negative Staphylococci), identified by culture from upper respiratory specimens.

Organism identification (Group A and MARCoNS identification)

Identification of recovered bacterial and fungal isolates, including Group A Streptococcus and MARCoNS identification, giving providers a clearer picture of the organisms detected.

Additional testing for positive cultures

For clinically significant bacterial and fungal isolates, RSM Diagnostics Lab offers additional testing to help guide treatment selection.

Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)

Positive bacterial cultures receive antibiotic susceptibility testing. Results are reported on clinically significant isolates to help guide antibiotic selection.

Natural Agent Sensitivity Testing

Positive isolates are tested against 10+ natural antimicrobial agents, offering supplemental in vitro data for providers who incorporate integrative or functional medicine into patient care.

Who should get tested?

This test may be useful in the evaluation of patients with the following presentations.

Clinical presentations

  • Recurrent or chronic sinusitis
  • Persistent nasal congestion or rhinitis
  • Chronic sore throat
  • Recurrent upper respiratory infections
  • Suspected bacterial or fungal colonization
  • Persistent symptoms despite prior treatment
  • PANS/PANDAS evaluation (pediatric acute-onset neuropsychiatric syndrome and its streptococcal-associated subtype)

Patients

Test details MARCoNS

Why test for MARCoNS?

MARCoNS (Multiple Antibiotic-Resistant Coagulase-Negative Staphylococci) testing is performed by culture, and can provide clinically useful information in patients with persistent upper respiratory symptoms or recurrent infections, particularly when standard therapies have not resulted in lasting improvement.

Who may benefit from testing

  • Recurrent sinus infections
  • Chronic rhinosinusitis
  • Persistent nasal congestion
  • Symptoms despite multiple antibiotic courses
  • Suspected chronic bacterial colonization
  • Recurrent upper respiratory infections
  • Persistent postnasal drainage
  • Patients being evaluated within integrative or functional medicine practices where chronic nasal colonization is part of the clinical assessment

Note: The association between MARCoNS and CIRS is not universally accepted. Current evidence is limited, and major infectious disease and otolaryngology guidelines do not recommend routine MARCoNS testing for all patients with chronic sinus disease.

Laboratory disclaimers

The following disclaimers apply to natural agent sensitivity testing and MARCoNS results.

Natural Agent Sensitivity Testing. Natural agent sensitivity testing is performed in vitro (laboratory testing) and is intended to provide supplemental information for healthcare providers. Laboratory susceptibility results may not directly predict clinical effectiveness or patient outcomes. Natural agent sensitivity results should be interpreted in conjunction with the patient's clinical presentation, medical history, and other diagnostic findings. This testing is not intended to recommend, endorse, or replace standard medical therapy or evidence-based clinical judgment.

MARCoNS Results. MARCoNS results should always be interpreted within the context of the patient's clinical history, symptoms, physical examination, and other diagnostic findings. Colonization does not necessarily indicate active infection, and detection alone does not establish disease causation. Natural antimicrobial sensitivity testing reflects in vitro laboratory activity and may not predict clinical effectiveness in individual patients.
